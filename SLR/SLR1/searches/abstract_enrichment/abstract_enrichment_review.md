In the recent past, the advancement of miniaturization and the revolutionary design philosophy have made nano-satellites the go-to platform for several commercial and scientific missions to low Earth orbit. The leverage for accommodating failures has thus reduced expeditiously, and analysis of mission-critical risks have assumed paramount importance to ensure the optimum performance of the system in its operating environment. However, from the Systems Engineering Methodology point of view, considerable nano-satellite projects continue to follow the conventional approach of Document-Based Systems Engineering. This approach involves the development of a Risk Response Matrix (RRM) to identify and categorize a risk based on its severity and likelihood. However, interpreting the risks associated with a nano-satellite through elaborate and esoteric documentation is maladroit and has quite a few pitfalls, the predominant ones being: the ineffective capturing of risks from a system point of view and a superficial understanding of how these risks affect other subsystems and subcomponents, apart from involving a substantial amount of book-keeping. To obviate these shortcomings, this paper expedites a novel idea of implementing a Model-Based Approach to assess and manage the risks faced by RVSAT-1, a student nano-satellite project, using SysML. The crux of the idea proposed is to represent the risks involved at the system and subsystem level as a model in SysML which depicts factors like risk severity and likelihood in a unique colour scheme based on a ‘risk cube display’. Certain aspects such as consequences, risk ownership, mitigation and contingency plans too can be represented lucidly. Though developed for RVSAT-1, a student nano-satellite, this approach can be utilized for other complex systems as well. The proposed model for risk management enables ease of access and optimization, allowing the systems engineers to focus on value added tasks and ensure that a balanced approach is adopted towards managing risks. Through these proposed risk models, unprecedented levels of systems understanding can be achieved through integrated analytics, coalesced with a model-centric technical baseline. They can be used to intuitively assess and manage risks with great rigor and precision, contrary to the conventional document-based approach.
