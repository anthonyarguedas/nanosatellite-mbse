The source script records an offset-free local `extracted_at` timestamp; those timestamps are reproduced below. The initial preflight timestamps are UTC. `L` is `--limit`; `P` is `--max-pages`.

## Refinement lineage

1. **Path-validation failure and recovery.** The first three planned pilots were blocked before any Scopus call by an unsupported macOS `realpath -m` flag. Each failure is preserved as parseable JSON with `total: null`, not misreported as zero results. The portable `cd … && pwd -P` validation was then used, and all three retries completed normally.
2. **Planned Stream A.** S-A1 yielded 3/3 explicit EPS-name records. S-A2 yielded 8/8 functional-EPS records, including power-system, energy-model, and toolkit terminology. Tightening the functional query to explicit MBSE/language terms (S-A3) and adding a structured-model concern/bridge (S-A4) yielded no new source records. Population-wildcard checks S-A5 and S-A2r1 reproduced S-A1 and S-A2 exactly, respectively. S-A1 and S-A2 are therefore retained as the concise, fully retrieved Stream-A set.
3. **Planned Stream B.** S-B1 was the intentionally broad explicit-MBSE/language baseline (74/74); its `cubesat*` refinement S-B1r1 returned 79/79 and added five records, so S-B1r1 replaces it. S-B2 was the structured-model/relation expansion (53/53). S-B3 adds `systems engineering`/`system engineering` to the relation block and uses `cubesat*`; it yielded 63/63, with seven records not present in the initial A1/A2/B1/B2 union. S-B4 is the direct wildcard refinement of S-B2 (58/58); it adds five to S-B2 and is retained as the robust B2 form. Neither retained B query requires EPS.
4. **Outcome and V&V probes.** S-V1 (1/1) and S-O1 (2/2) were valid, precise sensitivity probes but added no record outside the baseline. They were not used to remove or narrow any RQ1-capable record.
5. **Population sensitivity.** The plan-permitted `small satellite`/microsatellite probes were deliberately coupled to explicit EPS and MBSE blocks. S-A6 was a duplicate. S-A7 returned a PlanarSat power-first title and a SAR-data title, neither of which establishes an additional CubeSat/nanosatellite candidate from title metadata; neither is carried into the primary population final set.

## Source limitations and handoff cautions

- The Scopus search endpoint as mapped by the prescribed script supplied empty `abstract` and empty `authors` arrays for **360/360** retrieved rows across all successful runs. Titles, years, DOI where present, Scopus IDs, and citation counts are preserved, but later screening should enrich metadata or use full text rather than treat these blanks as evidence of absence.
- All API calls completed with numeric totals and no API/service failure. The only failures were the documented local preflight path-check failures, which made no request and were not counted as zero-hit searches.
- Language and document-type constraints are source metadata filters; later workflow should verify publication type/language where a record is ambiguous. No open-access, outcome, or V&V filter was imposed on the retained baseline streams.
- The 2026 Scopus index is a time-stamped snapshot; in-press metadata, document types, coverage of proceedings/chapters, and citation counts can change.
- This slice does not perform screening, retained-cache matching, citation chasing, cross-source deduplication, retrieval, or any legacy comparison.

## Stopping rationale

The planned Scopus syntax was validated with successful nonzero pilot responses in both streams; all planned A, B, V&V, and outcome probes were executed. The final set covers explicit EPS names, functional power/energy terms, explicit MBSE/language terms, structured-system-model terms plus engineering-relation terms, reference/digital-engineering lineages, and a deliberately EPS-free embedded-thread stream. Population-wildcard tests improved B recall where needed, while A wildcard reruns were identical. The high-specificity small-satellite sensitivity did not identify an additional direct target-population candidate. Recent precision and population refinements were otherwise duplicate-heavy, and all retained-query records were fully paginated/retrieved. Further Scopus keyword changes are therefore more likely to be redundant or increase off-topic burden than address an observed concept gap. Remaining recall risks are appropriately deferred to IEEE Xplore, supplementary discovery, full-text screening, and later citation chasing under the protocol.
